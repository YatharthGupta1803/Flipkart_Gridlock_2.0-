2. SPATIOTEMPORAL FEATURE ENGINEERING PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The dataset requires moving beyond standard tabular processing to capture real-
world traffic physics. We engineered a 38-dimension feature matrix:

* Deep Historical Memory (24H & 48H Lags): Traffic obeys strict daily and 
  multi-day cycles. We engineered a rolling lag mechanism by mathematically 
  shifting the training `day` matrix forward (Day+1 and Day+2). This maps exact 
  historical demand directly to the current query without self-reflection leakage.

* Micro-Spatial K-Means Hubs (Unsupervised): Standard geohashes are rigid 
  rectangles. To map organic city traffic, we deployed K-Means (n_clusters=150) 
  across the decoded coordinate space to identify precise micro-hubs and computed 
  the Euclidean distance of every grid to the urban epicenter.

* Trigonometric Temporal Constraints: Tree algorithms misinterpret 23:59 and 
  00:01 as numerical opposites. We applied sine and cosine transformations to 
  the hour and minute vectors, mapping time to a continuous geometric circle.

* Infrastructure Capacity Proxies: We generated a continuous `Traffic_Capacity_Index` 
  by scaling the `NumberofLanes` against a categorical `LargeVehicles` clearance weight.


3. LEAKAGE-FREE TARGET ENCODING (THE K-FOLD ISOLATOR)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To map the historical priority of individual spatial nodes without overfitting, 
we engineered an out-of-fold (OOF) target encoding loop.

By processing the training matrix through an isolated 5-Fold split, the historical 
mean demand for a specific `geohash` (and a custom `interaction_geo_time` string) 
was calculated EXCLUSIVELY using records the model was not currently evaluating. 
This provided a massive baseline signal while guaranteeing zero look-ahead bias.


4. MULTI-ENGINE ENSEMBLE CONFIGURATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Our predictive engine leverages a triplet of diverse, deep-growth gradient 
boosting frameworks to capture varying levels of feature interaction:

* LightGBM (Leaf-Wise Growth): Engineered for deep complexity with `num_leaves=256`, 
  uncapped `max_depth=12`, and `min_child_samples=10` to rapidly isolate rare 
  bottleneck conditions.
* XGBoost (Depth-Wise Regularization): Configured with `max_depth=9` to build 
  structurally conservative trees, acting as a mathematical counterweight to LGBM.
* CatBoost (Symmetric Topology): Configured with `depth=10`, excelling natively 
  at processing our highly-cardinal temporal/spatial interaction crosses.


5. META-OPTIMIZATION: NELDER-MEAD SIMPLEX BLENDING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Rather than estimating arbitrary ensemble weights (e.g., 33/33/33) or minimizing 
generic RMSE loss, the pipeline mathematically aligns with the leaderboard metric.

The OOF prediction arrays are passed into a SciPy Nelder-Mead simplex solver. 
The solver's objective function is hardcoded to minimize the negative score of 
the platform's exact metric: max(0, 100 * R2). This forces the solver to output 
the mathematically optimal blending ratios for this specific competition layout.


6. POST-PROCESSING & OPERATIONAL EXPORT LIMITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Before the final prediction arrays are streamed to disk, they pass through a 
rigorous physical boundary filter:

* Physical Target Bound Enforcements: Real-world traffic demand is a physical 
  measurement that cannot drop below 0% or exceed 100%. The pipeline applies a 
  strict NumPy clipping filter (0.0 to 1.0) to prevent the tree algorithms from 
  bleeding points on mathematically impossible negative trend predictions.

* Platform Schema Formatting: The final data structure is dynamically mapped 
  to output exactly 41,778 rows mapping perfectly to the required case-sensitive 
  system headers: "Index" and "demand". Standard Pandas indexing is omitted via 
  `index=False` to ensure instant grading compatibility with the evaluation server.
